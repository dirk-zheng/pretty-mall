### The same botanical name can represent different supply paths

When buyers compare botanical extracts from China, the visible product name may look identical while the supply path behind it is different.

One offer may come directly from an extraction manufacturer. Another may come through a distributor with local stock. A third may involve standardization, blending or repacking at a separate site.

None of these routes is automatically better or worse. But they should not be treated as equivalent before confirming:

- who manufactures the exact grade;
- where extraction, standardization and packing take place;
- whether the offered specification belongs to that grade and site;
- how the sample connects to the proposed commercial supply;
- what MOQ, packaging and lead-time conditions apply.

This is why we look beyond the plant name when reviewing an option from China’s cosmetic-ingredient supply chain. The useful question is not only “Do you have this extract?” but also “What exact supply route and grade does this offer represent?”

What is usually harder for your team to confirm: the manufacturing source, the exact grade or the commercial terms?

## 建议素材

- 首选：已脱敏的供应路径图，展示 `manufacturer → distributor/exporter → buyer`。
- 可选：同一植物名称下两份已脱敏报价的字段对比，只保留角色、地点层级、等级、MOQ 和包装。
- 不使用无法证明来源的工厂或实验室图片。

## 表述边界

- 不暗示所有植物提取物都经过多层贸易。
- 不把制造商直供表述为天然优于经销商供货。
- 不公开供应商名称、价格、联系人或未经授权的文件。
